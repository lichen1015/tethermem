The problem

One memory policy cannot serve two opposite needs.

Subject identity needs long-range access. Scene progression needs recency. Applying one frame-level memory policy to both creates the familiar identity–progression trade-off.

The real choice is not “remember or forget.” It is which query should read which part of memory.
RF

Rolling-Forcing

A short rolling context can preserve local continuity while repeatedly returning to the opening view.

Failure · stagnation
DF

Deep-Forcing

Long-range motion can continue even as spatial detail and trajectory coherence deteriorate.

Failure · incoherent motion
RAG

LongLive-RAG

Frame-level retrieval gives subject and scene queries the same historical context, limiting progression.

Failure · shared policy
TM

TetherMem

Query-aware routing keeps identity-bearing history available while scene queries favor recent states.

Route · subject ≠ scene

Evidence

Identity and progression can coexist.

Human preference distinguishes coherent scene development from raw motion. TetherMem occupies the high-identity, high-progression region, while strong motion alone is not sufficient.
